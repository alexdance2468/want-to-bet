How Want to Bet works

What is it?

Want to Bet is a place to back your opinions with fake money. You say something will happen, choose the odds you're willing to offer, and other people decide whether to bet against you. No real money is involved, and there's no login.

The pages

  • Offers: bets ready to settle, the open offers you can bet on, and the form to make your own offer at the bottom.
  • Odds over time: pick a bet to see a chart and table of every offer made on it, oldest to newest.
  • Leaderboard: everyone's net winnings, a head-to-head comparison of any two people, and the list of settled bets.

1. Pick a group

Everything on the site lives inside a group, and each group has its own bets and leaderboard.

  • Everyone (public) is the default group. Anyone can see it, and it's where you land if you don't join another group.
  • Create a group under Create or join a group to get a private space. You'll get an invite link to share.
  • Join a group by opening an invite link, or by pasting a link or code into the Join box. Your groups appear in the dropdown.

The invite link is the only protection, so only share it with people you want in the group.

2. Type your name

At the top of the page, type your name in the Your name box. This is the name shown on your offers and bets, and your browser remembers it for next time.

3. Make an offer

The form is at the bottom of the Offers page (or tap Make an offer at the top to jump to it). Choose what kind of bet it is:

  • A statement, for example "It will rain in Sydney on Saturday". You back it, and others bet against you.
  • A question, for example "Who will win the Melbourne Cup?". You give your own answer, other people add theirs, and everyone else bets against the answers they think are wrong.

Then fill in:

  • Odds: how much you'll pay out for every 1 someone bets against you.
  • Most you'll pay out: your total limit across everyone who bets.
  • Topic and subtopic: pick from the list, such as Sport then NRL. If yours isn't there, choose Other (type your own) and it will appear in the list for everyone in the group.
  • Betting closes (optional): after this time nobody can bet. Leave it blank for no closing time.
Example: You offer odds of 2 with a most-you'll-pay-out of 100.
  • Sam bets 10 against you. If you lose, you pay Sam 20. If you win, Sam pays you 10.
  • Up to 50 in total can be bet against you, because 50 × 2 = 100.

4. Bet against an offer

Find an offer or answer you disagree with, type how much you want to bet (any amount up to what's left), and click Bet. Several people can each take a piece of the same offer until the limit is used up. You can't bet against your own offer, and you can't bet once betting has closed.

Use the topic dropdowns above the open offers to show just one topic. They only list topics that have bets in your group.

5. Offer your own odds or add an answer

On a statement, click Offer your own odds to back the same statement at different odds. Your odds can be higher or lower than the others, and the person betting chooses whichever offer they like.

On a question, click Add your answer to back a different answer (or the same one at different odds). Each answer has its own odds and limit.

Everything added to a bet shares its topic and closing time.

6. Say who won

Once betting has closed (or straight away if there's no closing time), the bet appears under Ready to settle at the top of the Offers page. Anyone in the group can settle it, and the result is final straight away.

  • Statements: click the name of the winner, such as Alex won or John won. Each bet is settled separately.
  • Questions: click the correct answer, or None of these. Everyone who backed that answer wins from the people who bet against it, and everyone who backed a wrong answer pays out to the people who bet against theirs. All the bets on the question are settled together.

If nobody has bet on your offer yet, you can click Cancel offer instead.

The leaderboard and head to head

  • Leaderboard: everyone's net winnings across settled bets in the current group. It starts at 0, goes up when you win, and can go below 0 when you lose. There's no wallet or starting balance.
  • Head to head: pick two people to see every bet between them, who won each one, and who is ahead overall.

Good to know

  • It runs on honesty. Anyone in the group can declare a winner and the result can't be changed afterwards, so only settle a bet when you're sure of the outcome.
  • Names aren't locked. Anyone can type any name, so use names your group recognises.
  • It's all fake. The numbers are just for bragging rights.
